## Abstract

<!-- archive-abstract-start -->
Text data are increasingly used to measure economic conditions, expectations, policy communication, and other latent variables that are difficult to observe directly. In this talk, I discuss how information extracted from text can be incorporated into structural macroeconomic time-series models, and what it may contribute to our understanding of economic dynamics.
<!-- archive-abstract-end -->

## Speaker Bio

Minchul Shin is a senior economic advisor and machine learning economist in the Research Department at the Federal Reserve Bank of Philadelphia. He works at the intersection of empirical macroeconomics, forecasting, and real-time economic monitoring, building quantitative tools and empirical frameworks used in applied macroeconomic analysis. He also collaborates across teams on data- and technology-intensive projects.

Minchul's research combines structural macroeconometric modeling with advanced econometric and machine learning techniques to produce credible, policy-relevant inference. His work has been published in leading economics and statistics journals, including the *Review of Economic Studies*, the *Journal of the American Statistical Association*, and the *Journal of Econometrics*, among others. He is an associate editor of the *International Journal of Forecasting* and the *Journal of Applied Econometrics*.

He has a Ph.D. in economics from the University of Pennsylvania and master's and bachelor's degrees in economics from Sungkyunkwan University. He taught econometrics at the Wharton School of the University of Pennsylvania and the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign.
